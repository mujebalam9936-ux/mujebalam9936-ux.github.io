Christmas math activities for kindergarten that still teach real math

Low-prep Christmas math activities and centers for kindergarten: counting to 10, numbers to 20, and adding and taking away within 10, plus no-print ideas.

December in kindergarten is a balancing act. The children are excited, the schedule is full of interruptions, and you still want the last three weeks before break to move counting and early addition forward. The activities below keep the holiday feeling but put real kindergarten math at the center of every one: counting to 10, reading numbers to 20, and adding and taking away within 10.

Start with what your class needs

Before choosing anything Christmas-themed, check where most children are:

  • Still counting objects to 10? Pick activities where counting is the task, like the flip books below (K.CC.B.4, K.CC.B.5).
  • Recognizing numbers 1 to 10 and starting to add? Color by number with two levels lets one picture serve both groups (K.CC.A.3, K.OA.A.5).
  • Adding within 10 confidently? Move them to subtraction and mixed facts within 10, still with a picture to reveal.

The theme is the wrapping paper. The skill is the present.

Christmas counting activities: flip books that move

Children color small number pictures (page 3 shows 3 ornaments, page 4 shows 4), cut the cards apart, mix them up, and then count to put them back in order. Staple the edge and flip: the ornaments fill the tree like a little cartoon.

Why it works for kindergarten: the order comes from counting the objects, not from reading a printed number, so children have to count accurately to make the animation work. It also gives the fine-motor practice that kindergarten needs in December: coloring small spaces, cutting straight lines, lining up a stack. Print the pages on cardstock, or glue the cards onto index cards, and the books flip much better.

For a calmer first try, give PreK or younger kindergarteners only the first page of each book: the cover and pages 1 to 5 make a small 1 to 5 book on their own.

Christmas color by number for kindergarten

A color by number page in kindergarten usually does one of two jobs: number recognition (find the 7, color it green) or addition facts within 10 (solve 4 + 3, then color). Here is how a level B space works: the space shows 4 + 3; the child solves it, finds the crayon labeled 7 in the key, and colors the space. A wrong answer usually shows up as a space in the wrong color, which the child can catch by comparing with the finished key.

When the same picture comes in both levels, you can hand level A to children still learning numerals and level B to children who are adding, and the whole class reveals the same Santa or snowman. Nobody's page looks "easier".

A December morning work routine

The weeks before break are when a predictable start to the morning matters most. A daily page with the same boxes every day (sound it out, two sight words, number of the day, a few math problems, partner letters and a sentence to read and copy) lets children start on their own while you take attendance and handle the notes from home. The holiday art keeps it from feeling like a drill, and because the layout never changes, children who were absent can jump straight back in.

Christmas math centers with no printing at all

Not every center needs paper. These five use things most classrooms already have:

  • Ornament counting jars. Put 1 to 10 small pompoms in clear cups with number cards on the side. Children count and match (K.CC.B.5).
  • Wrap the number. Write the numbers 1 to 10 on small boxes. Children add that many stickers or bows to each box.
  • Ten-frame trees. Draw a ten frame on green paper. Children place counters as "lights" and say how many more make 10 (K.OA.A.4).
  • Gingerbread take-away. Start with 8 paper gingerbread people. Tell a story where some get eaten. How many are left? Children act it out and then write the number sentence (K.OA.A.2).
  • Line up the stockings. Children order numbered stockings 1 to 20 on a string. Then take one away while they close their eyes and ask which number is missing.

Making it work in the busy weeks

  • Prepare one week at a time. Print the color by number pages and flip books on Monday and store each center in its own tub, with a picture label so children find it without reading.
  • Keep the crayon set small. Put only the colors on the key in each tub.
  • Use the answer keys. Children enjoy checking their own picture against the colored key. That is self-assessment, and it frees you for small groups.
  • Save the flip books for a calm afternoon. Cutting and stapling take adult help the first time; once the class knows the routine, the second book goes much faster.
  • Plan for the half days. Keep one folder of extra color by number pages ready for the days when the schedule changes at short notice.

Looking ahead to January

After break, winter themes keep the same skills going without changing routines: the same flip book idea with penguins and hot cocoa, and morning work that continues sight words and numbers to 20. Children walk back into a classroom where they already know what to do, which makes the first week of January much calmer.

Questions teachers ask

What math should kindergarten practice in December?

Kindergarten math in December usually centers on counting and writing numbers to 20, comparing numbers, and addition and subtraction within 5 and then within 10. Holiday activities work best when they practice exactly that.

How do I keep Christmas activities inclusive?

Mix Christmas pages with winter themes such as snowmen, penguins and hot cocoa, so every child has an option that fits their family. Winter pages also work well in the last week before break.

Are color by number pages real math?

They are when every colored space needs a solved problem or a read number to know its color. The coloring is the reward for the math, and the finished picture checks the answers.
